# Objections and landmines

Purpose: the five objections heard on the call and in replies, the response to each, and the landmine questions that expose the cost of the incumbent. The pattern and numbers come from the measured battlecard of a product that sits on top of an incumbent tool; treat them as the shape of the argument and compute your own per-unit figures with the prospect.

## The frame in one line

Augment, do not replace. The product plugs into the incumbent tool; the real competitor is the status quo plus the incumbent's own bolted-on automation.

## The objection table

| The prospect says | Respond with | Then |
|---|---|---|
| "We already have a tool" / "we just renewed" | "Good, keep it. This plugs into it and takes the part your team does by hand. Your investment stays." | Landmine 1 |
| "Our tool already has automation for that" | "Its automation is generic and priced into the licence. This one is dedicated to that loop, priced per unit, and running in days. We can measure it on 20 units." | Landmine 2 |
| "No budget" / "too expensive" | "There is no licence to fund. It is per unit: the measured case was 40 per hand-qualified ticket against 2 with the agent, 12.50 per handled chat against 1. Your number will differ; payback is in weeks. Start with a pilot." | Ask what a unit costs them today; never quote a price |
| "We are mid-migration" | "Then this is the moment: it sits on the new instance and absorbs the surplus during the migration. When is cut-over?" | Dated nurture to cut-over if no demo before |
| "We do not plug third-party tools into our systems" | "Understood. It starts in read-only or suggest mode: it reads, proposes, a person decides. Nothing is written until you switch it on." | Offer the compliance pack from regulatory-to-selling-points |

Three occurrences of the same competitor name in one segment inside 30 days is a pattern, reviewed at the weekly session. One call is an anecdote; three is data.

## The four differentiators, in the order to use them

1. Plugs into the incumbent: zero rip and replace.
2. Per-unit cost against an opaque per-user licence (the measured incumbent: roughly 59 per user per year in cloud, custom quotes, 3 to 5 times more on premise).
3. Days to value, not months: connection in days, where a simple change in the incumbent took 2 to 3 days and a consultant.
4. Start in read-only or suggest mode, then widen.

Never lead with a return-on-investment percentage. The measured battlecard kept its 350 to 600 percent figures for the full autonomous vision out of every cold conversation as unverifiable; they belong to the second meeting, with the prospect's own numbers.

## Landmine questions

Real questions, asked with curiosity, that make the prospect say the cost out loud.

| Landmine | What it exposes | Running example phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| "How long does a simple change take in your current tool today?" | The 2 to 3 days and the consultant dependency | "If you want to change one routing rule in the fleet tool, how long until it is live?" |
| "Have you costed a ticket / a pick / a handling error?" | The absence of a per-unit number, which opens the per-unit argument | "Do you know what one mis-routed ticket costs you, or one pick during peak congestion?" |
| "How many minutes does your team spend qualifying and routing one item by hand?" | Hidden labour cost | "Per ticket at L1, per pallet at receiving?" |
| "Does your reporting give you what you want without a workaround?" | The reporting pain of the incumbent | "Can you get cost per pick per zone out of it?" |
| "Where are you in the migration before the deadline?" (only with a known end of life or contract end) | Urgency | "Your desk tool version reaches end of support at the date you mentioned; where does the migration stand?" |

One landmine per call. Two is an interrogation.

## Worked example, clean

A CIO at a French mid-market insurer: "We have the incumbent, and its own automation module." Response: keep it, this sits on top, per ticket, days to run. Landmine: "How long does a simple change take today?" Answer: "About a week with our integrator." The CIO books a demo on 20 real tickets.

## Worked example, edge

A plant manager says "we do not connect anything external to the WMS, policy". Response: read-only mode first, a person executes; the compliance pack covers data residency and machine-safety declarations. He agrees to a demo if the security lead attends; the caller books a joint slot and notes it in the handoff pack.

## Worked example, gone wrong

Third call in the month where a 3PL names the same incumbent fleet software. The caller answers each one well and logs nothing about the pattern; the weekly review misses it because the objection field was free text with three spellings. Fix: a closed competitor list in the CRM, a count per segment per 30 days, an alert at 3. The next week the alert fires and the segment's angle moves to "what the incumbent's routing cannot do in peak".
