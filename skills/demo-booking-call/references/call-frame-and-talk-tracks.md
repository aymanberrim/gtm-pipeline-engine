# Call frame and talk tracks

Purpose: the five-part frame of the qualifying call, timed, then two talk tracks for the running example. The words are a pattern to adapt to the signal, never a script to read.

## The frame, 7 to 9 minutes

| Part | Time | What happens | Exit test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 0 to 30 seconds | Name, one sentence referencing the resource taken or the exact reply. Ask for 6 minutes | They say yes or give a better time |
| 2. Situation | 30 seconds to 2 minutes | "How is that handled today, by whom, with what tool" | You can describe their current loop in one sentence |
| 3. Problem and implication | 2 to 5 minutes | "Where does it break", then "what does that cost per shift, per week, per ticket" | A pain in their words plus a unit of cost they named |
| 4. Light qualification | 5 to 7 minutes | Authority (who signs), urgency (a date or driver), budget (a line exists or not) | A decision on whether the demo is worth it |
| 5. Book | 7 to 9 minutes | Two slots proposed, one taken, invitation sent on the line, receipt confirmed | Accepted invitation before hanging up |

Talk ratio: the caller speaks under 40 percent of the time in parts 2 and 3. The product gets one sentence at the transition to part 5, as the reason for the demo, not as a pitch.

## Talk track 1: warehouse-robotics vendor from Shenzhen, calling a plant manager

Context: a 40-person vendor of autonomous mobile robots entering Europe. The lead is the plant manager of a 350-person consumer-goods distribution centre in the Netherlands, who took the benchmark and clicked the "cost per pick" row in email 2. Registry mandate: none. Tenure ratio: 8 years at a 12-year-old site, 67 percent, probable decision-maker.

Hook: "Hello, Wei here, from the team that sent you the fleet-utilisation benchmark. You opened the cost-per-pick row, so I wanted your reaction to it. Do you have 6 minutes?"

Situation: "How is picking organised on the site today: what runs the routing, the WMS or a separate fleet tool?" (The answer confirms or corrects the stack.)

Problem: "Where does it slow down: congestion in the aisles, replenishment, the peak weeks?" Then: "Have you costed a pick, all in?"

Implication: "If congestion costs you the 12 percent you mentioned in peak, what does that mean in temporary staff per season?"

Authority: "When the site adds equipment, who signs: you, or is it a group decision?"

Urgency: "Is there a date this needs to be better by?"

Transition and book: "The robots plug into your existing WMS and take the congested part of the loop, without replacing anything. The useful next step is 30 minutes on your own layout with our integration lead. I have Tuesday at 10:00 or Thursday at 14:30, which one?" "You should have it now, can you see it?"

## Talk track 2: AI-agent platform from Austin, calling a head of operations or a CIO

Context: a Series-A platform that qualifies and routes tickets on top of the incumbent service-desk tool. The lead is the head of customer operations at a 1,200-person Nordic telecom, who replied "our L1 spends its day re-routing, who would I talk to". Registry mandate: none. Tenure ratio: 2 years at a 25-year-old company, 8 percent, so a prescriber. The CIO likely signs.

Hook: "Hello, Dana here. You wrote that your L1 spends the day re-routing tickets and asked who to talk to; that is me. Do you have 6 minutes?"

Situation: "How many tickets a day come into L1, and which service-desk tool routes them today?"

Problem: "When routing misses, what happens: the ticket bounces between teams, the SLA slips? How often?"

Implication: "Have you costed a ticket that gets qualified by hand? The measured battlecard priced that loop at 40 per ticket against 2 with the agent; what is yours?"

Authority: "If this went ahead, who signs: you, or your CIO?" "Would it make sense to have your CIO in the demo so we do it once?"

Urgency: "Is there a driver, a renewal of the desk tool, a headcount freeze, a migration?"

Transition and book: "The agent sits on your current desk tool and takes the qualification and routing; it starts in suggest mode, nothing is changed without a person. The next step is 30 minutes on 20 of your real tickets. Tuesday 11:00 or Wednesday 15:00, and I will put your CIO on the invitation if you send me the address now." Invitation sent on the line.

## What went wrong on a real-shaped call

A caller reaches an operations director at a 3PL, opens with two minutes on the company and the robots, hears "we are happy with our current setup", says "I will send you the deck", and hangs up with no date. No hook to the benchmark he took, no situation question, no landmine. Correct replay: hook on the benchmark row he opened, a situation question on peak congestion, the landmine "how long does a simple route change take in the current tool", then a demo slot or a dated nurture at day 30 with the note "happy with setup, peak in November".
