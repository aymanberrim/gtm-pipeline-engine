# Three-email sequence

Purpose: the sequence in its imposed order, deliver then value then ask, with two worked versions for the running example, the signal table that decides who gets called, and what never triggers a call. The copy rules are the segment-then-write rules: role-first opener, one CTA, no price, human voice, 1:1 layer on top of the template.

## The sequence

| Email | Day | Job | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, deliver | 0 | The promised resource, delivered unconditionally | Opens by naming the recipient's role. Zero pitch. The link is the resource, nothing else. Sent the day of the engagement peak. |
| 2, value | +3 | One insight tied to their pain plus one proof number | Under 50 words. A different angle from email 1. Link to the resource or a related tool. |
| 3, ask | +7 | The soft demo proposal | One CTA. Residual value in the last line for those who do not reply. |

The order is not negotiable. These people raised a hand; the first thing they receive is what they asked for, or the trust is gone before the pitch starts.

## Worked version A: warehouse robotics, fleet-utilisation audit

Email 1, day 0

Subject: your fleet utilisation audit

As the head of intralogistics, you asked for the 12-row audit we run on a site before quoting a fleet. Here it is: the spreadsheet, plus the one-page note on how to read the three rows most sites fail.

Link to the audit.

If a row is unclear on your site, reply and I will explain it.

(1:1 layer: name the site type or a fact from their profile in the first sentence. Zero pitch. The only ask is a question about the resource itself.)

Email 2, day 3

Subject: the row most sites fail

Row 7, mission wait time between WMS release and robot dispatch, is where utilisation goes: on the last 9 sites we audited, the median wait was 41 seconds per mission. Under 10 is reachable without new hardware.

Link to the method note.

(37 words. One insight, one number from your own audits, no ask.)

Email 3, day 7

Subject: row 7 on your own data

If row 7 came out high on your site, I can show in 15 minutes how the dispatch layer plugs into your WMS and what the wait becomes on a week of your own mission logs. Would a slot next week suit? If the fleet is run day to day by someone else on your side, the same 15 minutes is theirs to take.

(One CTA: the demo ask. The last line is residual value, offering the same slot to the rung-2 owner if the reader delegates, without adding a second ask.)

## Worked version B: AI SaaS, ticket-triage benchmark

Email 1, day 0

Subject: the ticket-triage benchmark you asked for

As the owner of the service-desk tool, you asked for the benchmark: triage time and misrouting rate across 40 European service desks by ticket volume. Here it is, with the method on page 2.

Link to the benchmark.

If your numbers sit outside the ranges, reply and I will tell you what usually explains it.

Email 2, day 3

Subject: what the misrouting rate hides

In the benchmark, desks above 12 percent misrouting spend more analyst time re-reading tickets than resolving them. The desks under 5 percent all had one thing in common: the categorisation happened before a human opened the ticket.

Link to the two-page comparison.

(37 words. One insight, one number, no ask.)

Email 3, day 7

Subject: your misrouting rate, on 20 of your tickets

If your rate sits above the 12 percent line, I can show in 15 minutes what the agent does on 20 of your own tickets when it plugs into your ticketing tool. A slot next week? If the queue is owned by someone else on your team, the same walk-through is theirs.

## The signal table

| Signal | Strength | Action | Mobile bought? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product or resource test (ran the audit, used the calculator, started a trial) | Strongest | Hot, call within 2 hours, alert | Yes, now |
| Reply | Strong | Classify the reply, act immediately; positive strong books, positive weak qualifies timing | Yes, if in ICP |
| Click on a sequence link | Real | Move to "to call", call within 24 hours | Yes, if in ICP |
| Resource page visit (tracked) | Weak-positive | 5 points, stay in sequence | No |
| Open | Noise | 3 points, stay in sequence or nurture | No |

## What never triggers a call

- An open alone, however many times. Opens are inflated by mail clients and security scanners; a person who opened email 1 four times may be a proxy.
- Three opens across three emails: still noise.
- A reaction on the original post without any sequence signal: that is the entry ticket, not intent.
- A partner referral with no signal yet: it enters at high priority in the sequence, and the first call waits for the partner's introduction or a signal, whichever is first.

## Nurture and reactivation

- "Not now" replies get a dated nurture state with a wake-up date; the wake-up is a value touch, never "have you thought about it".
- A lead in nurture who clicks or replies moves straight back to "to call".
- "Lost, refused" becomes reactivable after 90 days (default to tune); timing changes.

## Failure table

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch in email 1 | Reply rate on email 1 collapses, unsubscribes | Deliver only |
| Email 2 over 50 words | Second email reads like a pitch | Cut to one insight, one number |
| Two CTAs in email 3 | Replies answer the question and skip the demo | One CTA |
| Calling on opens | Cold calls to confused people | Call on test, reply, click only |
| Mobile bought at day 0 | Credits spent on non-responders | Buy on signal |
| Sequence starts a week after the post | Open rate halves | Day 0 on the engagement peak |
