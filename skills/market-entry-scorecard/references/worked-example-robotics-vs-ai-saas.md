# Worked example: robotics vendor versus AI SaaS vendor

Purpose: the same rubric run for two invented vendors with opposite outcomes, plus one run that went wrong. Numbers are illustrative; the method is what transfers.

## Vendor A: a 40-person warehouse-robotics vendor from Shenzhen

Product: AMR fleet for case picking, ACV 180,000, 8 install days on site, integrates with the incumbent WMS. Footprint: one pilot at a grocery distributor whose sister site is in the Netherlands, one advisor in Germany.

Hard gates: CE marking in progress, self-declared via internal production control for this AMR, technical file and testing dated 14 weeks out (a notified body would apply only if the configuration falls in a category that requires one; confirm the route with counsel); passes conditionally, so the 90-day plan targets a signed pilot with delivery after the mark. Operator logins only; DPA drafted. Counsel confirmed no export-control issue.

Candidates: Netherlands, Germany, France, Poland.

| Criterion (weight) | NL | DE | FR | PL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Addressable accounts (20) | 5 (210 sites, registry plus association list) | 10 (1,300) | 7 (480) | 5 (190) |
| Buying accessibility (15) | 10 | 5 | 3 | 5 |
| Regulatory load (15) | 5 | 3 (safety demands untested) | 3 | 5 |
| Competitive density (10) | 3 | 0 (5 funded competitors with references) | 3 | 7 |
| Proof proximity (15) | 7 (pilot's sister site) | 3 (advisor) | 0 | 0 |
| Channel availability (15) | 7 (one integrator asked to resell) | 5 | 5 | 3 |
| Cost to serve (10) | 5 | 3 | 0 | 3 |
| Total | 61.5 | 47 | 33.5 | 39.5 |

Netherlands leads by 14.5 points; no tie-break needed. 61.5 sits in the 55 to 69 band: partner-led mode, cheap proof target. Selector row 1 fires (hardware, 8 install days, no local technicians): partner or integrator. The integrator that asked about reselling names the pilot's sister site as the joint account at signature, so time to first reference is 14 minus 6, about 8 weeks.

90-day plan: day 30, 160 NL sites in the CRM with the WMS detected on at least 120; day 60, integrator agreement signed with the named joint account and 8 first meetings; day 90, one signed pilot, delivery after the CE mark. Switch trigger: no named joint account by day 45 after signature moves to the second integrator. Germany starts only after the NL pilot signs.

## Vendor B: a Series-A AI-agent platform from Austin

Product: AI agents for customer-service teams, ACV 45,000, 1 remote install day, processes customer conversations, so personal data and the AI Act tier matter. Footprint: three inbound UK customers, one partner in Ireland, no EU customers.

Hard gates: AI Act memo written, use case outside the high-risk categories per counsel, transparency obligations apply (a transparency-obligation system, often called limited-risk). The customer-to-vendor transfer runs on the vendor's EU-US Data Privacy Framework self-certification (or SCCs Module 2 controller-to-processor between customer and vendor); SCCs with the cloud provider cover only the onward transfer; EU-region hosting available; DPA exists. All gates pass.

Candidates: UK, Ireland, Netherlands, Germany, Nordics (scored as one cluster).

| Criterion (weight) | UK | IE | NL | DE | Nordics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Addressable accounts (20) | 10 (1,400) | 3 (120) | 7 (520) | 10 (2,100) | 7 (610) |
| Buying accessibility (15) | 10 | 10 | 7 | 5 | 10 |
| Regulatory load (15) | 7 (pack accepted by 3 UK buyers) | 7 | 5 | 3 | 5 |
| Competitive density (10) | 0 | 5 | 3 | 3 | 5 |
| Proof proximity (15) | 10 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Channel availability (15) | 7 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 7 |
| Cost to serve (10) | 10 | 10 | 5 | 3 | 5 |
| Total | 81 | 61.5 | 47.5 | 45.5 | 57 |

UK leads at 81, enter now. But the CEO asked about the EU, and the UK sits outside it: UK references do not answer an EU buyer's residency question. Shortlist: UK now (direct sales remote-first, row 6, marketplace as procurement path); for the EU, Ireland (61.5) and the Nordics (57) are within 5 points. Tie-break 1, proof proximity: Ireland 5 versus Nordics 0. Ireland is the EU beachhead, EU-region hosting on, the Irish partner naming the first joint account.

Opposite of Vendor A: Vendor B's largest market by count (Germany) scores near the bottom on accessibility, regulation and cost, a third-wave market.

## The run that went wrong: a Bangalore vendor of AI safety-monitoring cameras for factories

Product: computer vision that flags unsafe worker behaviour on the shop floor, ACV 70,000, 3 install days, processes images of identifiable workers.

What happened: the team scored Germany first on account count (1,900 plants) and skipped the gates because "cameras are just hardware". In month four, three pilots stalled at works-council consultation, and counsel placed the use case in a high-risk AI Act category as a workplace-management system, with obligations the vendor had no roadmap for; biometric-adjacent features drew a hard stop at two accounts. Regulatory load should have scored 0 and the AI Act gate should have failed pending a roadmap.

Corrected run: gate fails until the high-risk roadmap exists (12 weeks). Then regulatory load scores 3 across the EU, consultation-heavy countries lose a point on accessibility, and proof proximity decides: a pilot in a Polish plant of a customer's group scores 7. Poland leads at 58, partner-led through the group's integrator, with the works-council briefing pack (see the regulatory-to-selling-points skill) as the opening artifact.

Lesson: the skipped gate cost two quarters; the scorecard would have cost two days.

## Questions when the two patterns blur

1. Must a person install a unit on site? Then proof proximity and channel availability decide, not account count.
2. Does the product see people? Then regulatory load and accessibility decide, and the gate runs with counsel before scoring.
3. Will the second country's buyers read the first reference? If not, it is not the second wave.
