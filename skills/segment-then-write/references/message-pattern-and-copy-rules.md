# Message pattern and copy rules

Purpose: the fixed structure of a first email and its two follow-ups, the rules that are not up for debate, and two worked messages for the running example. The pattern is the measured one; the words are transposed.

## Structure of the first email

| Part | Rule | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | The specific failure the reader lives with, in their words. No product name, no "quick question", no first name. | 4 to 9 words |
| Paragraph 1 | Name the recipient's role, then the loop they are stuck in. "As the [role], ..." The role adapts to the group and is refined to the exact title in the 1:1 layer. | 2 or 3 sentences |
| Paragraph 2 | What plugs into the tool they already run and takes that part of the loop off their hands. Name the incumbent tool when stack detection gave it as certain. | 2 sentences |
| Paragraph 3 | The 15-minute offer on their own data (their tickets, their fleet logs, their pick lines). One CTA. | 1 or 2 sentences |
| Signature | Name, role, one line. No banner, no tracking pixel on the first send, few links. | |

Total under 120 words. The 1:1 layer (2 or 3 facts about the person or company) sits in paragraph 1 or 2, never as a bolted-on first line.

## Sequence CTA variation

The target of the CTA never changes: the demo. The phrasing changes at every step.

| Step | Day | Opener | CTA phrasing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Role, then loop | "A slot this week?" |
| 2 | +3 | "Coming back on..." plus one new fact or number | "I can show it on 20 of your own [tickets, missions, pick lines] in 15 minutes." |
| 3 | +7 | Short, one line of residual value | "If this sits with someone else on your side, who should I write to?" |

Step 2 and 3 are follow-ups in the same thread. Only step 1 carries the role-first opener.

## Rules that are not negotiable

- Role before pain. Never attack with raw pain ("your warehouse loses money every shift"), never open with a sender self-introduction ("I am the founder of..."). The measured team rejected the founder-intro version explicitly.
- One open question or one CTA per message, never both.
- No price in cold. The cost comparison (the manual cost of the task versus the automated cost) belongs to the demo, where it can be defended on their data.
- No unverifiable claims in cold. A 350 to 600 percent ROI figure was ruled too aggressive for a first email in the source work; a concrete per-unit number was kept for the second conversation.
- Human voice. No jargon, no em-dashes, no "I hope this finds you well", no "I am reaching out", no "I would love to", no "synergy", no "leverage", no "revolutionise".
- Exclude consultancies, integrators, competing vendors. Different motion.
- Plain text, few links on the first send, verified-safe addresses only.

## Forbidden openers, with the replacement

| Forbidden | Why | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| "I am the founder of a robotics company from Shenzhen" | About the sender | "As the head of intralogistics at a three-site network, ..." |
| "Your pickers are wasting 30 percent of their time" | Raw pain, unproven | "As the picking manager, part of every shift goes to..." |
| "Quick question" | Says nothing | The failure, in the subject |
| "We help companies like yours" | Generic | Name the tool it plugs into |

## Worked message 1: warehouse robotics, fleet or WMS administrators at European 3PLs

Subject: missions that die between the WMS and the robots

As the WMS administrator, every automation cell on the floor means another integration you keep alive by hand: a mission fails to dispatch, the exception lands on your desk, and the fix is a manual re-push while the picker waits.

Our fleet layer plugs into the WMS you already run and takes that part. It reads the order wave, dispatches to the robots, catches the failed mission and retries it before anyone notices.

If this is a familiar afternoon, I can show it in 15 minutes on a week of your own exception logs. A slot this week?

(1:1 layer for one recipient: the site opened a second mezzanine last quarter, the company posted a job for a second WMS administrator, the WMS vendor is known from stack detection. Two of the three go into paragraph 1 or 2.)

## Worked message 2: AI SaaS, service-desk tool owners at mid-size European companies

Subject: tickets that bounce twice before the right team

As the owner of the service-desk tool, a large part of your analysts' day goes into the same loop: read the ticket, decide incident or request, categorise it, route it. When the routing misses, the ticket bounces between teams and the resolution time takes the hit.

Our agent plugs into the ticketing tool you already run and takes that part. It reads the incoming ticket, decides incident or request, sets service, category and urgency, and sends it to the right queue.

If that loop is real for your team, I can show what it does on 20 of your own tickets in 15 minutes. Would a slot this week work?

(1:1 layer: the company posted a "service desk team lead" role, the tool owner published a post on ticket backlog, the ticketing vendor is certain from stack detection.)

## The mediocre version, annotated

"Hi, I am the CEO of an AI company from Austin. Companies like yours lose thousands of hours to manual ticket triage. Our AI cuts triage time by 90 percent and costs only 2 euros per ticket. Do you have 30 minutes next week, or should I send you our deck?"

Sender-first opener; raw unproven pain; a percentage the reader cannot verify; a price in cold; two CTAs; no role, no tool, no loop.

## Failure table

| Failure | Detected by | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Subject with product name | Open rate under 35 percent after 60 sends (default to tune) | Subject as the failure |
| Two CTAs | Replies answer the question, ignore the demo | Keep the CTA, drop the question |
| Price in cold | "Too expensive" before any demo | Keep the price for the demo |
| Merge-field-only sends | Replies starting with "who are you" | Enforce the 2 or 3 fact layer |
